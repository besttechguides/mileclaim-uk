# HMRC Approved Mileage Rates — Tax Year 2026/27

**Period:** 6 April 2026 – 5 April 2027  

**Source:** GOV.UK — [Travel — mileage and fuel rates and allowances](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/rates-and-allowances-travel-mileage-and-fuel-allowances/travel-mileage-and-fuel-rates-and-allowances)

> **Not tax advice.** Rates and rules can change. Always verify on GOV.UK.

---

## AMAP rate table (2026/27)

| Kind of vehicle | First 10,000 business miles in the tax year | Each business mile over 10,000 |
|-----------------|--------------------------------------------:|-------------------------------:|
| **Cars and vans** (incl. electric / hybrid) | **55p** | **25p** |
| **Motorcycles** | **24p** | **24p** |
| **Bicycles** (incl. electrically assisted pedal cycles) | **20p** | **20p** |

### Passenger payments (cars and vans only)

| | Rate |
|--|-----:|
| Per passenger who is a fellow employee, per business mile (journey also work for them) | **5p** |

Passenger payments are separate from the vehicle mileage rates. There is no tax relief if you receive less than 5p (or nothing) for carrying passengers.

---

## Note on earlier years

For tax years **up to and including 2025/26**, the car/van rate for the first 10,000 miles was **45p** (then 25p). From **6 April 2026** the first-10,000 car/van rate is **55p**. Motorcycle and bicycle rates are unchanged at 24p and 20p.

## Worked MAR example — employer 45p vs HMRC 55p

**Scenario (illustrative only):**

- You drive a **car** for work in 2026/27.  
- You do **1,000** qualifying business miles in the year (all within the first 10,000).  
- Your employer pays **45p** per mile.  
- HMRC’s approved rate for those miles is **55p**.

| Step | Calculation | Amount |
|------|-------------|-------:|
| A. Approved amount (AMAP) | 1,000 × 55p | **£550** |
| B. Employer mileage payments | 1,000 × 45p | **£450** |
| C. Shortfall (potential MAR basis) | A − B | **£100** |

**What this means in plain English:**

- You already received £450 from your employer (that part is typically within / compared against the approved framework as paid).  
- The **£100 shortfall** is what Mileage Allowance Relief is about — tax relief on that gap, **not** a cash payment of the full £550 from HMRC.  
- How much tax you get back depends on your tax situation (e.g. whether you pay tax at the basic rate). This pack does **not** calculate your personal refund.

**If the employer paid 55p (or more) for those miles:** shortfall on those miles is usually **£0** — no MAR for that mileage.

**If miles go over 10,000:** use **25p** for car/van miles above 10,000 when working out the approved amount.
